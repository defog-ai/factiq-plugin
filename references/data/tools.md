# FactIQ Tool Reference

The full contract of the three company-research tools. `skills/factiq/SKILL.md`
holds a short row for each tool and points here. Read a tool's row before the
first call to that tool. Each row is complete: it names every parameter,
target, filter, paging rule, and evidence rule.

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `search_company_filings` (`company`, `query?`, `concept?`, `concept_id?`, `search_target?`, `report_type?`, `fiscal_year?`, `fiscal_period?`, `quarter_basis?`, `metric_class?`, `segment?`, `date_from?`, `date_to?`, `active_only?`, `format?`, `limit?`, `offset?`) | The central tool for company filings. Deterministic (no model) search over the structured facts and report metadata in one company's filed reports, for every company FactIQ covers: US SEC filers (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, plus 20-F/40-F/6-K for foreign filers, and for companies that went public recently the S-1/F-1 registration statement and the 424B4 or 424B1 final prospectus) and companies listed in Germany (annual, half-year, Q1, and Q3 reports, values in EUR — e.g. `company="BAS"` for BASF SE), the UK (`ULVR` for Unilever plc), France (`MC.PA` for LVMH), Switzerland (`NESN` for Nestlé), and India (the Nifty 50 companies, from their NSE quarterly results and annual reports — `RELIANCE` for Reliance Industries; an Indian company's Q4 results hold its audited full year). Use an exact ticker; a share-class sibling (GOOGL for GOOG) resolves to the same filer; a German, UK, French, or Swiss company resolves by its local ticker, full name, or LEI, and carries its exchange suffix (MC.PA, BA.L, ROP.SW) when the bare symbol also belongs to a US filer; an Indian company resolves by its NSE symbol (RELIANCE, M&M), the same symbol with `.NS`, its BSE scrip code, or its full name; an ambiguous name comes back with `possible_matches`. Start with `search_target="coverage"` to see which report types, periods, and metric classes exist. Then set `concept` to one metric name (`"revenue"`, `"net income"`) to get that concept's values across periods, or use `search_target="metrics"` to list stored concepts and `"facts"` for reported values. `query` is a lexical text search across concept names, source labels, aliases, and segment names; narrow with `metric_class` (`financial`, `ifrs`, `segment`, `geography`, `product`, `kpi`, `apm`, `guidance`, `offering_terms` for the IPO price, shares offered and proceeds), `segment`, `report_type` (`annual`, `quarterly`, `half_year`, `10-K`, `10-Q`; `424B4` with `search_target="commentary"` reads only a final IPO prospectus), `fiscal_year` + `fiscal_period` (`2025`, `Q3`), or `date_from`/`date_to`. A year with a quarter (`2022`, `Q3`) is read as the calendar quarter of that name: pass the year and the quarter as the user named them, and do not convert them to the company's fiscal labels. For a company whose fiscal year does not end in December (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia) the result is the company quarter that covers most of that calendar quarter, and `quarter_reading` and `period_note` name it. Set `quarter_basis="fiscal"` only when the user's words name the company's fiscal quarter ("fiscal Q3 2022", "FY22 Q3") or when the year and quarter come from a FactIQ result, such as the coverage listing. A calendar quarter that has not ended, or that the company has not reported yet, is read as the fiscal quarter, and the note says so. `fiscal_year` alone or with `FY` is the company's fiscal year. Each value's `period_names` gives the fiscal period it covers and its calendar quarter; state the period dates with the value. When a `concept` name finds no stored concept, the error lists the company's concepts whose names share words with it under `concepts_sharing_words`; when a concept has no value under the filters, the note lists the concepts that do and the periods the concept holds. To select one listed concept, call again with its `concept_id`; a listed concept can measure something else, so check its name first. When the error says a ticker is not stored, retry with the company's name or SEC CIK; if that finds nothing, the company's filings are not in FactIQ, so never use the values of a company with a similar ticker. A ticker the company had before a rename still finds it, and `company_note` says so. `search_target="officers"` (US SEC filers) returns the people in office now — chief executive, chief financial officer, chief accounting officer, other executive officers, and directors — and the appointments and departures the company reported in Form 8-K (Item 5.02), newest first. Each current line gives the title, an `is_interim` mark, the start date when a report gave one, and whether the line comes from the last annual report or from a later 8-K; each change gives its effective date, a `verbatim_quote`, and the `source_url` of the 8-K. One person can hold two positions (an interim chief executive who stays chief financial officer has two lines). `officer_coverage` gives the date of the annual list and the dates of the 8-Ks read; read the result's `note` for what the list does not hold (directors elected at an annual meeting, foreign filers that file no 8-K). Use this for "who is the CEO/CFO of X" and "when did X change its CEO" instead of your own memory, which can be out of date. Every result is a tree: company → metric class → concept → series → period. With `format="json"`, filing/fact nodes retain the report URL and add a standardized `source_link`; exact-ticker metrics/facts misses may fall back to standardized statements with no filing evidence. `format="pretty"` returns a rendered text tree instead of JSON. When a company reports the same concept twice in one filing, the second copy is labeled "Reported line 2" — never add it to the first. A result holds at most `limit` rows (max 50). When a `facts`, `metrics` or `filings` result has `has_more` true, call the tool again with the same parameters and `offset` set to its `next_offset`, and repeat until `has_more` is false; the rows have one fixed order, so the calls neither repeat nor skip a row. `coverage`, `commentary`, `risk_changes` and `officers` return an error when `offset` is above 0. Every `metrics` result carries `series_counts`: the classes and breakdown axes this company has, with the number of stored series in each. `series_counts` covers the whole company and ignores the filters of the call, so read it before you say that a class or a breakdown is not held. For joins or aggregations across companies over the same archive, use `run_sql` on the `filings` schema (open to every account; tables in `references/data/schemas.md`). |
| `search_earnings_transcripts` (`query`, `search_target?`, `ticker?`, `company_name?`, `quarter_filter?`, `claim_family?`, `section?`, `detail?`, `limit?`, `offset?`, `date_to?`) | Lexical (not semantic) retrieval over atomic, quote-anchored earnings-call rows, plus the stored text of one call in pages (`search_target="transcript"`). Tickers go in `ticker` and company names in `company_name`; pass one of the two, never both (both ignore case; a name in `ticker` is still read as a name, and the response says so). `company_filter` is the old name of `ticker` and still works. Companies listed in India carry the NSE suffix (`RELIANCE.NS`, `TCS.NS`); a plain NSE symbol is also accepted and reported in `read_as_exchange_symbol`, but a symbol that is also a US ticker (`INFY`) selects the US listing, so pass `INFY.NS` for the NSE one. Companies listed in Switzerland carry `.SW` (`NESN.SW`, `ROP.SW`) and follow the same rules (`ROP` is Roper Technologies; Roche is `ROP.SW`); their half-year calls are Q2 and full-year calls Q4. For a non-empty `query`, strict websearch matches rank above an automatically broadened loose partial-match OR-of-tokens tier, so lower-ranked rows may match only some terms; trigram fallback runs only when full-text search returns no rows. Inspect every row for support and retry concise company-native vocabulary (`"capital expenditure"`, `"capex"`, segment names) before concluding lexical silence. For one-call notes, first use `search_target="coverage"`, choose its exact returned `latest_period`, then browse `claims` with `query=""`, that ticker + `quarter_filter`, `detail=true`, and a deliberate limit; fetch `pressure_points` with the same ticker and quarter. The browse is capped, not a promise of a complete call. To read the whole call, use `search_target="transcript"` with one ticker and its exact `quarter_filter`: `section` is `qa` (default; up to 10 exchanges per page, each with the asker, the firm, the question and every answer with its speaker and role), `prepared_remarks` or `all` (turns in the order spoken, each with its whole text); while the result says `has_more`, call again with `offset=next_offset`. `date_to` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) keeps only the calls held on or before that date, for a question about a past date. Claim and pressure rows include `transcript_id`, `source_block_index`, `qa_turn_id`, and `source_link`; `source_link.source_label` names the company or ticker, fiscal period, and earnings call transcript, never the ingestion vendor. Quote only `verbatim_quote` or the stored text of a transcript page (`question`, the `text` of an answer, the `text` of a turn), never `canonical_statement`, and give the speaker's name. A transcript page returns no source URL: name the call with its ticker and fiscal period and do not invent a link. Preserve any `[…]` omission marker in `verbatim_quote` exactly; `canonical_statement` is normalized, and neither `analyst_hypothesized` nor `mgmt_declined_to_confirm` is a management assertion. For filed actuals use `search_company_filings`; for formal targets use `sec_guidance`. Full target/filter reference and workflows: `references/report-patterns/earnings-intelligence.md`. |
| `search_media_appearances` (`query`, `search_target?`, `company?`, `person?`, `sort?`, `appearance_type?`, `claim_family?`, `date_from?`, `date_to?`, `detail?`, `limit?`, `offset?`, `institution?`, `country?`, `show?`, `channel?`, `company_filter?`) | Two kinds of source: (1) what company executives, investors, fund managers, analysts, economists, journalists, and other guests said on podcasts, TV interviews, and at conferences outside earnings calls; (2) documents that central banks and monetary authorities published from late 2025 onward: speeches, testimony before legislatures, policy statements, meeting minutes, press-conference transcripts, press releases, blog posts, interviews, and reports from the Federal Reserve Board and the regional Reserve Banks, the European Central Bank, the Bank of England, the Bank of Japan, the Reserve Bank of India, the People's Bank of China, the Bank of Korea, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, Taiwan's central bank, and the national central banks of the euro area and other EU members (a document in another language yields English paraphrases; its title may stay in the source language). Use it for what Fed, ECB, or other central-bank officials said, not only for what executives said. **Search with one to three key words** in `query` (`"inflation outlook"`, `"export controls"`) and put the speaker in `person`, the publisher in `institution`, and the kind of document in `appearance_type`. A returned row must contain most of the query words, so a long question returns few or no rows; retry with the speaker's own vocabulary or a synonym before concluding there is no coverage. Retrieval is deterministic and lexical over precomputed public-safe paraphrases; **no serving-time model** interprets or expands the query. Strict full-text matching (all query words) runs first, loose full-text matching (most of the query words) only when strict finds no candidates, and trigram matching only when both are empty. Claims cover listed and unlisted companies (OpenAI, DeepSeek, MiniMax), institutions (central banks, regulators), and whole markets and industries, not only the speaker's own employer. Targets are `search` (default claims + passages blend), `claims`, `passages`, `pressure_points`, `appearances`, and `coverage`. `sort="relevance"` ranks lexical score before publication date; `sort="newest"` ranks publication date before lexical score. `company` takes comma-separated tickers and company names together (`"NVDA,OpenAI,Federal Reserve"`): a stored ticker matches by ticker, and any other value matches the name of the company, institution, or organisation a section is about, ignoring punctuation, a leading "The", and corporate suffixes (`"NVIDIA Corp."` finds NVIDIA; `"Alphabet"` also reaches GOOGL rows). Each result records `company_tickers`, `company_names`, `company_matched`, and `company_unmatched`; a name that matches nothing returns no rows plus up to five possible matches under `company_unmatched`, so retry with one of them. For `appearances` and `coverage`, each value is also matched exactly, upper-cased, against the entity references of claims (products, rivals, partners such as `"CUDA"`) and listed under `company_references` when found only there, so alternate spellings can return different rows. `company_filter` is the old name of `company` and still works, but do not pass both. `company` matches what a claim is about, not who published it (`company="European Central Bank"` also returns a Bank of England speech about the ECB). To select a publisher, use `institution`, a case-insensitive substring of the publishing institution (`"Bank of Japan"`, `"Bundesbank"`), or `country`, the ISO code of its jurisdiction (`"US"`, `"IN"`, `"EU"` for the European Central Bank); either one restricts every target to official documents, and `coverage` then returns one `UNATTRIBUTED` row with the document count, date span, and claim count. `person` is a case-insensitive speaker-name substring; `show` is a case-insensitive podcast show-name substring (`"Odd Lots"`, podcast episodes only) and `channel` a case-insensitive YouTube channel-name substring (`"Bloomberg Television"`, YouTube videos only); when more than one of `show`, `channel`, and `institution` is given, rows that match any of them are returned; `coverage` lists the stored show and channel names for each company. `appearance_type` values are `podcast`, `tv_interview`, `conference`, and `other` for interviews, and for official documents `speech` (also blog posts and opening remarks), `tv_interview` (published interviews), `statement` (also implementation notes), `minutes` (also summaries of opinions), `press_conference`, `testimony`, `hearing`, and `other` (press releases, reports, projections). `claim_family`, inclusive `date_from`/`date_to`, and `detail` narrow further. `limit` is one page of 1-50 rows and `offset` skips that many ranked rows; every result carries `has_more`, and `next_offset` when more rows follow. Dates are the publication/upload date, not necessarily the event date; an official document also carries `event_date`, the date of the meeting, speech, or hearing (FOMC minutes are published about three weeks after the meeting). `claim_family` makes blended search claims-only, is invalid with `passages`, and requires matching claims for catalog targets. Finding rows expose `result_kind`, `canonical_paraphrase`, speaker/topic/video metadata, relevance, and `source_url`: a timestamped YouTube URL for a video, the episode page for a podcast, or the document page for an official document (a PDF transcript links to the page, `...pdf#page=4`); for an official document, `channel` is the institution name. `appearances` returns video-level metadata, attribution, matching-claim count, URL, relevance, and `institution`, `document_kind`, and `event_date` (null on interview rows); `coverage` returns company-level structured corpus counts and date/channel inventory. `detail=true` adds normalized claim/attribution fields plus `institution`, `document_kind`, and `event_date` to finding rows, but never raw transcript text or evidence spans; claim-only fields remain null on passages and detail does not change catalog rows. Never put `canonical_paraphrase` in quotation marks or claim it is verbatim; follow the timestamped or document source when exact wording or tone matters. In a press-conference transcript, a row whose speaker is `Questioner` is a journalist's question, not the institution's position. Empty-query behavior and the complete workflow are in `references/report-patterns/media-intelligence.md`. |
